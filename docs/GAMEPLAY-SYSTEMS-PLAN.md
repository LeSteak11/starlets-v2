# Starlets — gameplay, training, battle, and retention plan

**Status:** Proposed product plan for discussion; no gameplay implementation authorized

**Scope:** Post-onboarding repeatable play. The separate mobile/onboarding workstream owns the first-session presentation, tutorials, visual direction, and creative production.

## 1. Product thesis

Starlets cannot rely on rarity alone. A card must change what the player can do with a Starlet, reveal something desirable about it, or help advance a goal. The game should create attachment through repeated use: discover a Starlet, choose it, train it, try different cards, win short challenges, and see it grow.

The recommended product is a **character-collection game with short tactical PvE battles**. It is not a full competitive card game and does not begin with PvP.

The central relationship is:

> The Starlet is the character you raise. Its cards are collectible battle styles that change how it plays.

The player keeps one persistent version of each Starlet. Level, Bond, battle record, and growth belong to that Starlet. Equipping another owned card changes its artwork, Signature action, and passive effect without creating a second creature or resetting progress.

## 2. Design goals

The system must:

1. Give every card a functional or expressive reason to exist.
2. Create a satisfying two-to-ten-minute session.
3. Offer meaningful choices without requiring genre expertise.
4. Make a favorite Common Starlet viable throughout PvE.
5. Reward daily return without punishing a missed day.
6. Provide specific goals alongside random discovery.
7. Keep early content and animation requirements achievable.
8. Avoid an economy in which every action needs a different currency.
9. Delay PvP until the roster, balance data, and audience justify it.
10. Keep purchases out of battle power and randomized rewards during validation.

## 3. The complete player loop

### Moment-to-moment loop

1. Choose a Starlet and equip one of its owned cards.
2. Enter a short activity: scan, training session, or battle.
3. Make a small number of readable decisions.
4. Receive cards, Stardust, shards, XP, Bond, or goal progress.
5. Improve the chosen Starlet or move closer to a specific card.
6. See the next clear objective before leaving.

### Daily loop

1. Claim the daily Observatory transmission.
2. Review three compact daily objectives.
3. Complete one training session with a chosen Starlet.
4. Spend Sparks on one or more Signal Lock discoveries.
5. Complete one short PvE battle path.
6. Open a ready pack or advance toward the next one.
7. Spend, craft, train, or change the active card.

A player can have a worthwhile visit by doing only the first three items. Completing everything is optional.

### Weekly loop

- Complete five daily transmissions on any five days; they do not need to be consecutive.
- Defeat the week's three-stage Constellation Challenge.
- Advance a chosen Starlet's Bond objective.
- Complete or substantially advance one binder page.
- Earn one guaranteed choice reward from a restricted card pool.

### Long-term loop

- Discover every Starlet on a planet.
- Collect each Starlet's battle styles.
- Raise favorite Starlets through Level and Bond milestones.
- Earn cosmetic Star Rise forms and Star Marks.
- Complete numbered binder pages and seasonal sets.
- Build teams for increasingly difficult PvE constellations.
- Display and share favorite Starlets and cards.

## 4. What cards do

Every numbered card is an equipable **Style Card** for its species. The player's Starlet may equip one Style Card at a time.

Each card defines:

- Its artwork, title, rarity, and finish
- One Signature action
- One simple passive trait
- A tactical identity such as aggressive, defensive, control, recovery, or utility
- Optional story or Bond content unlocked on first acquisition

The Starlet provides:

- Persistent Level and XP
- Persistent Bond
- Base HP, Power, Guard, and Speed profile
- Core identity and species-specific basic action
- Battle history, Star Rise, and earned Star Marks

### Card-power rule

Rarity must not mean strictly stronger battle power. Higher-rarity cards may be more specialized, visually impressive, or mechanically unusual, but every Style Card uses the same overall power budget. A Common can be the best option for a particular enemy or team.

Example Mossbun styles:

| Card                | Role        | Signature                                         | Passive                                         | Why the player wants it |
| ------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Mossbun Base        | Balanced    | Verdant Echo: deal damage and gain 1 Guard        | Steady: first Guard each battle is stronger     | Reliable starter style  |
| Garden Drift        | Sustain     | Dew Rest: heal and gain Focus                     | Patient: healing improves below half HP         | Long battles            |
| Moss Light          | Control     | Glowspore: weaken the enemy's next action         | Listener: reveals one extra enemy intent        | Tactical safety         |
| The Listening Field | Burst setup | Resonance: store power, then release it next turn | Stillness: bonus if the previous turn was Guard | High-skill payoff       |

Finish remains presentation. A foil and matte version of the same designed style should not have different battle strength. Under the current catalogue, finishes are fixed per card; duplicate finish variants should not be introduced until the content model is intentionally revised.

### Collection rewards

- First card of a species: discovers and unlocks that Starlet.
- Second distinct card: unlocks style switching and a short character scene.
- Complete numbered species line: unlocks a species-specific frame, Star Mark, or animation.
- Complete a nine-card page: grants a choice of one missing numbered card from that page plus a page cosmetic.
- Complete all 27 numbered cards: grants a set display treatment and one selected Secret encounter or deterministic Secret path.
- Secrets are prestige styles/cosmetics and count toward prestige completion, not basic set completion.

## 5. Starlet growth

Growth has two tracks with different jobs.

### Level

Level represents use and modest combat growth.

- Range: 1–20 for the first release.
- Earn XP from first discoveries, duplicate grants, training, and PvE battles.
- Level increases base battle values gradually, with most power available early.
- Recommended total difference between level 1 and level 20: approximately 25%, not multiples of starting power.
- PvE encounters scale by progression band so new content remains playable without invalidating growth.
- Equipped cards never have separate levels.

This creates visible improvement without making a newly discovered favorite useless.

### Bond

Bond represents relationship and unlocks expression and utility rather than raw dominance.

Five Bond ranks:

| Rank             | Requirement          | Unlock                                                           |
| ---------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 · Met          | Discover the Starlet | Use in activities and battles                                    |
| 2 · Familiar     | 3 Bond points        | Profile line and idle animation                                  |
| 3 · Trusted      | 8 total              | Second training result choice and species frame                  |
| 4 · Close        | 16 total             | Character scene and cosmetic Star Rise option where available    |
| 5 · Constellated | 30 total             | Signature Star Mark and premium display pose earned through play |

Bond points come from the first meaningful activity with that Starlet each day, selected story milestones, and species objectives. Repeating a trivial action indefinitely does not farm Bond.

### Star Rise

Star Rise should be a visual growth state, not a mandatory power evolution. It can change the in-app model, effects, profile art, or animation while preserving species identity and every equipped Style Card.

Do not require every Starlet to have multiple forms at launch. Introduce Star Rise only when the creative team can support a polished form and when Bond has proved valuable.

## 6. Training

Training is a short daily activity that builds XP and Bond while teaching battle decisions.

### Structure

- Three free training sessions per day across the account.
- One session lasts approximately 30–60 seconds.
- Choose a Starlet, then choose one of three drills.
- Each drill contains one simple interaction or battle scenario.
- The first meaningful session with a Starlet that day awards one Bond point.
- All sessions award XP; strong performance gives a small Stardust bonus.
- Unused sessions do not accumulate.

### Initial drills

1. **Power Drill:** time two taps or choose the best moment to strike. Teaches attack timing and awards Power-oriented battle practice.
2. **Guard Drill:** respond to two telegraphed enemy actions. Teaches defense and awards a small Stardust bonus for correct reads.
3. **Focus Drill:** choose the correct sequence to charge and use a Signature action. Teaches the battle resource.

Drills do not permanently allocate stat points. Permanent stat allocation creates irreversible mistakes, dominant builds, and balance overhead. Level provides modest universal growth; player expression comes from Style Cards and team choice.

Training sessions are a daily allowance, not a purchasable power gate during validation. Extra sessions may later come from earned objectives, but should not be sold until the game has a fair competitive policy.

## 7. Battle system

### Format

- PvE first.
- Three Starlets per team.
- One active Starlet at a time; switching uses the turn.
- Battles last at most six rounds or approximately two minutes.
- The enemy's next action is telegraphed.
- Each active Starlet chooses one action per round.
- The player wins by defeating the enemy team or having more remaining team vitality at the round limit.

Three-Starlet teams make collecting more relevant than one-on-one combat while keeping the screen readable and the content burden controlled.

### Actions

Every Starlet has four choices:

1. **Quick:** reliable low damage; generates 1 Focus.
2. **Power:** higher damage; costs 1 Focus.
3. **Guard:** reduces incoming damage and may trigger card passives; generates 1 Focus.
4. **Signature:** supplied by the equipped Style Card; normally costs 2 Focus.

Focus begins at 0 and caps at 3. This creates an understandable rhythm: build, read the enemy, then spend.

### Enemy intents

Initial intents:

- Attack: incoming direct damage
- Heavy Attack: larger damage, clearly signaled
- Guard: reduced incoming damage
- Charge: prepares a stronger next action
- Recover: restores health or removes a condition
- Switch: changes the active enemy

The player should be deciding against visible information, not guessing unseen dice.

### Stats

Only four displayed values:

- HP: survival
- Power: outgoing damage
- Guard: damage reduction
- Speed: resolves ties and influences switching

Use small whole numbers in the interface. Hide formulas during normal play.

Proposed starting formula:

`damage = action power × attacker Power ÷ defender Guard`, followed by a narrow ±5% variation.

The narrow variation prevents every turn from being identical without overriding decisions. No critical-hit system in the first battle release.

### Conditions

Launch with no more than four:

- Exposed: next damaging action received is stronger
- Shielded: next damaging action received is weaker
- Slowed: loses Speed advantage on the next resolution
- Renewing: heals a small amount after the next action

Conditions last one action or one round and never stack beyond one instance. Card text should stay short enough to understand without a glossary.

### Cores

Grove, Tide, and Flare should remain identity at first and gain **soft team synergy**, not a damage triangle.

Example: fielding three different Cores grants the team +1 starting Focus; fielding two matching Cores strengthens one corresponding support effect. This encourages team construction without teaching type charts or making favorite characters bad into specific opponents.

### Failure and retries

- Losing consumes no premium currency.
- The player keeps a smaller XP reward and any objective progress already earned.
- Retry is immediate.
- After two losses, offer an optional tactical hint or easier difficulty for that encounter.
- Never offer a purchase immediately after a loss.

## 8. PvE activities

### Constellation Trail

The main repeatable battle path. Each planet contains a map of short nodes:

- Standard battle
- Training challenge
- Choice event
- Recovery node
- Elite battle
- Planet guardian

A run contains three to five nodes and can be paused between nodes. Winning grants XP and Stardust. First completion grants a fixed card or card-choice reward. Repeat completion grants smaller resources and weekly progress.

### Daily Signal Challenge

One battle with a clear modifier, such as “Guard twice” or “Win with two Grove Starlets.” It should encourage trying different cards and teams. The first win awards daily-objective progress; repeated farming is unnecessary.

### Species Missions

Persistent objectives that create attachment:

- Win three battles with Mossbun active.
- Use Verdant Echo five times.
- Finish a battle after protecting an ally.
- Discover two Mossbun styles.

These unlock profile entries, frames, poses, and Star Marks. Each Starlet needs a small authored mission track before it feels like a character rather than a collectible ID.

### Constellation Challenge

The weekly three-stage encounter. It requires three Starlets, has visible enemy behavior, and grants a deterministic card-choice reward on first weekly completion. Difficulty may scale, but the base reward must remain accessible without purchases.

## 9. Daily and streak structure

### Daily Observatory transmission

One claim per local calendar day. It grants a small fixed reward and reveals that day's three objectives.

### Daily objectives

Offer three, with progress shared across normal play:

1. Activity objective: complete one training session.
2. Discovery objective: complete one Signal Lock or open a pack.
3. Character objective: complete one battle with a specified Starlet, Core, or Style role.

Completing one objective makes the day worthwhile. Completing all three grants a modest bonus, not an essential exclusive reward.

### Return track

Use a seven-claim track rather than a fragile consecutive streak.

- Missing a day pauses the track.
- Five claims within a calendar week complete the weekly attendance objective.
- Claim 3 awards shards.
- Claim 5 awards a pack.
- Claim 7 awards a restricted card choice or cosmetic token.
- The next reward remains visible.

This creates anticipation without turning real-life interruption into lost progress.

### Suggested session sizes

- 30 seconds: claim and view goals.
- 2 minutes: claim plus training.
- 5 minutes: training plus one battle or Signal Lock.
- 10 minutes: complete daily objectives and manage collection.
- Longer: Constellation Trail, team experimentation, Binder, and weekly challenge.

## 10. Economy

Use three earnable resources at first.

### Sparks

Purpose: pace Signal Lock discoveries.

- Keep six starting, eight regeneration cap, one per 90 minutes during testing.
- Signal preview remains free; committing to Signal Lock costs one.
- Training and battles do not cost Sparks.
- Do not create a separate battle-energy currency.

### Stardust

Purpose: flexible account utility and expression.

Initial sinks:

- Purchase one Spark: proposed 150 Stardust, with a daily limit during testing.
- Unlock basic display frames and profile treatments: proposed 300–1,000.
- Retrain a daily objective once: proposed 50.

Do not use Stardust to buy permanent battle stats. Avoid pack-timer skips until return behavior is understood.

### Shards

Purpose: deterministic card acquisition.

- Duplicates award universal shards according to rarity.
- The player selects a missing numbered card as a target.
- Crafting grants the exact selected card.
- Secrets use a separate completion path and are not routine crafts initially.

Proposed starting costs for testing:

| Rarity    | Duplicate shards |              Craft cost |
| --------- | ---------------: | ----------------------: |
| Common    |                1 |                       8 |
| Uncommon  |                2 |                      18 |
| Rare      |                3 |                      40 |
| Legendary |                8 |                     100 |
| Secret    |               25 | Not initially craftable |

These are test values. Simulate collection completion before locking them.

### XP and Bond

XP and Bond are progression measures, not spendable currencies. Do not add upgrade materials, move tokens, food currencies, or multiple shard types during the first battle release.

## 11. Packs and targeted acquisition

Packs remain the broad random-discovery source. Signal Lock provides active play and species targeting only once zone or signal selection exists. Crafting provides the deterministic backstop.

Recommended acquisition triangle:

- **Packs:** broad surprise
- **Signal Lock:** active species pursuit
- **Crafting:** exact-card completion

The current scanner chooses from the entire weighted roster, so it is not truly targeted. Add selectable planet zone signals after onboarding: one free rotating zone each day, with the other zones visible and selectable using normal Sparks. The preview may reveal the zone and signal characteristics without revealing the species.

Give a new player one tutorial pack after the introductory sequence. Thereafter, retain the 12-hour timer and two-pack cap for initial testing.

The Secret guarantee must be redesigned before implementation. Recommended rule:

> Every pack advances a pack-specific counter by one. A Secret is guaranteed in the fifth slot of pack 20 if none appeared earlier.

Do not count individual cards or Signal Lock grants toward this counter. This promise is understandable, enforceable, and does not conflict with scanned species that lack Secret cards. Display progress only after pack 12. Exact threshold remains a tuning value.

## 12. Motivation structure

At all times, the player should see goals at three distances.

### Now

- Complete this training drill.
- Win this battle.
- Finish this Signal Lock.
- Reveal the next pack card.

### Next

- Earn six more shards for Garden Drift.
- Complete today's three objectives.
- Raise Mossbun to Bond 3.
- Fill the last two slots on Moon Garden.

### Later

- Defeat Lunara's guardian.
- Complete all numbered cards.
- Reach Bond 5 with a favorite Starlet.
- Earn a Constellated Star Mark.
- Build a team that clears the weekly challenge.

The interface should favor specific named goals over percentages without context.

## 13. Monetization boundary

Do not implement real-money purchases until accounts, server-authoritative entitlements, parental/age handling, restoration, and durable analytics exist.

During validation, mock only deterministic offers:

- Named cosmetic frame
- Named profile or display treatment
- Fixed bundle with fully listed contents
- Season cosmetic track after retention is proven

Do not sell battle stats, training sessions, accuracy, wider timing windows, retries after losses, or randomized paid packs. Do not make a paid card mechanically superior to obtainable cards.

## 14. Content required for a battle vertical slice

The smallest meaningful slice requires:

- Three playable Starlets: Mossbun, Emberpanda, and Novafox
- Two functional Style Cards per Starlet
- Four battle actions per active Starlet, with Quick/Power/Guard shared where possible
- Six Signature actions and six passive traits
- Three simple enemy types and one guardian
- Four conditions
- Three training drills
- One three-node Constellation Trail
- One daily challenge template
- Three short species-mission tracks
- Basic hit, guard, switch, defeat, and victory effects
- UI portraits or battle poses for the three Starlets

This proves whether cards create meaningful play before building mechanics for all 30 cards.

## 15. Implementation order and gates

### Gate 1 — rules prototype

Implement the battle engine as pure logic using placeholder visuals. Include actions, Focus, switching, enemy intents, six-round resolution, rewards, and deterministic tests.

Decision gate: are choices readable and does equipping a different card change the preferred strategy?

### Gate 2 — three-Starlet vertical slice

Add the six Style Cards, three enemies, guardian, training drills, and first Trail. Integrate the approved onboarding handoff where it touches first battle/training unlocks.

Decision gate: does the player form a preference for a Starlet or card because of play?

### Gate 3 — progression

Add Levels 1–20, five Bond ranks, species missions, and rewards. Run progression simulations before exposing final values.

Decision gate: does growth create anticipation without making low-level Starlets unusable?

### Gate 4 — daily structure

Add the transmission, three daily objectives, seven-claim return track, and weekly challenge. Add persistent analytics before external testing.

Decision gate: can a short visit feel complete, and is tomorrow's purpose obvious?

### Gate 5 — economy and collection integration

Add crafting targets, shard costs, Stardust sinks, page rewards, and targeted zone signals. Fix pack guarantee and reward reporting as part of this integration.

Decision gate: do random rewards reliably turn into visible progress toward a chosen goal?

### Gate 6 — content expansion

Only after the vertical slice is accepted, author Style mechanics for the remaining cards and battle content for the remaining five Starlets.

## 16. Required analytics

Record:

- Session start/end and anonymous player ID
- Daily claim and objective progress/completion
- Training selection, result, and chosen Starlet
- Battle start, team, equipped cards, actions, result, rounds, retries, and difficulty changes
- Signal Lock quality and granted card
- Pack opened and per-slot outcome
- Card equipped, crafted, favorited, and displayed
- Level and Bond milestone
- Page/species/set completion
- Every resource source and sink

Core product measures:

- Tutorial completion
- First battle completion
- Players who equip a second Style Card
- D1, D3, and D7 return
- Daily objectives completed per active player
- Battles per session and retry rate
- Percentage with a named craft target
- Time to second distinct card for a favorite species
- Page completion and weekly-challenge completion

## 17. Acceptance criteria for the gameplay vertical slice

- A new player can explain why two cards for the same Starlet are different.
- Switching cards changes at least one meaningful battle decision.
- A battle can be completed on a phone in under two minutes.
- Enemy intent is understandable before the player chooses.
- A favorite Common Starlet can clear the slice without a rare-card requirement.
- Training teaches battle rules and advances the selected Starlet.
- Losing does not remove progress or present a purchase prompt.
- A duplicate advances an exact chosen-card goal.
- The player can identify a useful activity available now, a goal available next, and a longer objective.
- Return progress pauses rather than resets after a missed day.
- The system can measure whether players return and whether they change Style Cards.

## 18. Decisions to settle before coding

The plan recommends answers to most structural questions. The remaining product decisions are:

1. Confirm three-Starlet teams rather than one-on-one battles.
2. Confirm telegraphed turn-based PvE as the first battle mode.
3. Confirm one equipped Style Card per Starlet.
4. Confirm rarity as specialization/prestige rather than direct power.
5. Confirm three account-wide training sessions per day.
6. Confirm the non-consecutive seven-claim return track.
7. Confirm Secrets remain outside routine crafting and basic set completion.
8. Confirm a pack-specific guarantee, proposed at pack 20, replacing the current per-card counter.

Once these are locked, the developer can create a technical battle brief and data schema without inventing product rules during implementation.
